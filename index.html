THE PIVOT • JUNE 30

Stonington → Nantucket, two days early

Tuesday morning in Stonington, the planned progression changed. With worsening weather expected, Grey Expectations went straight to Nantucket. The forecast called for patchy morning fog and afternoon thunderstorms. Two dense fog banks still developed en route, but the boat reached Nantucket before the afternoon weather. What happened that day Bookings, backup plans and two fog encounters

June 29–30, 2026 • All times Eastern Daylight Time. Reconstructed from Dockwa confirmations, marina messages and receipts, the Garmin track, the trip notes and the 12:52 PM photo.

A shorter route meant rearranging the stops

John and Kirsten were in Stonington on Monday night. The planned progression still included two intermediate overnight stops: TPG Conanicut Marina in Jamestown on June 30, then Falmouth Town Marina on July 1. With worsening weather ahead, the trip notes describe the decision to use Tuesday’s available window and go straight to Nantucket. The forecast in those notes called for patchy morning fog and afternoon thunderstorms.

The reservations show how that change came together. Conanicut was requested at 3:22 PM on June 29 and confirmed at 3:53 PM for June 30–July 1. Falmouth’s July 1–2 booking had already been confirmed on June 4. There was also an existing Nantucket mooring reservation. The route notes describe arriving two days earlier than the planned progression; the Dockwa mooring dates themselves changed by one day, from July 1 to June 30.

Tuesday morning: secure the destination, keep an alternative

  1. 7:46 AM
    Nantucket says an early arrival is welcome.

    Nantucket Moorings replied with instructions to check in on VHF 68 for an assignment. The updated confirmation brought arrival forward to June 30, with departure still listed as July 6. An additional payment of $116.88 was recorded that morning.

  2. 8:15–8:29 AM
    Falmouth is adjusted, then restored.

    At 8:15 AM, Falmouth changed the booking to June 30–July 1; a message at 8:16 AM confirmed that adjustment. At 8:27 AM, it returned to July 1–2. At 8:29 AM, the marina said it still had room for arrival that day and asked John to check back if he chose to come in. That left a documented alternative available while the Nantucket reservation was in place.

  3. By 9:18 AM
    The boat is underway from Stonington.

    The first sample in the website’s June 30 chart is at 9:18 AM. The completed leg covers 91.4 nautical miles. The destination arrangement was in place before that recorded underway sample; the intermediate bookings were canceled later.

The window was usable, but it was not fog-free

Two dense fog encounters forced substantial reductions from the usual 26–29 kt cruise. The chart marks approximately 10:35 AM–12:00 PM for the first interval, lasting about 85 minutes with a median speed of 9.7 kt. After a faster stretch, the second interval runs approximately 12:47–1:38 PM, about 51 minutes with a median speed of 8.7 kt. The fog photograph taken at 12:52 PM falls inside that second interval.

These are reconstructed slow-speed intervals aligned with the two reported fog encounters, rather than instrument measurements of visibility. Together they account for about 2 hours 16 minutes of reduced-speed travel. The track tells us when the boat slowed; the trip account and photo explain why.

Along the way: close out the unused reservations

  1. 12:28 PM
    Conanicut is canceled.

    The June 30–July 1 Jamestown booking was canceled. At 12:29 PM, the marina replied that it was sorry to hear the boat would not be coming and invited a future visit.

  2. 1:51 PM
    Falmouth is canceled.

    The restored July 1–2 booking was canceled after the second chart-marked fog interval. Its refund was processed that evening.

  3. By 2:24 PM
    The track has reached Nantucket Harbor.

    The final chart sample is at 2:23:56 PM in the harbor. The trip notes report arrival before the afternoon weather and worsening conditions afterward. Those notes describe the outcome of the weather call; the Dockwa emails establish the booking sequence.

Conanicut’s $291.40 payment resulted in a $276.40 refund, leaving a $15 cancellation fee. Falmouth refunded its full $235 payment. Nantucket’s updated booking listed departure on July 6, but the actual voyage resumed toward Newport on July 5. Reserved dates and the route actually traveled are kept separate here.

The emails preserve the marina replies and reservation transactions. They do not capture every phone call, onboard discussion or forecast check. The sequence above does not assign an exact time to the decision to continue to Nantucket beyond what those records establish.
